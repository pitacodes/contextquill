# Automation playbook

ContextQuill is designed to reduce content effort without removing human judgment. The best setup automates discovery, drafting, reminders, and already approved schedules — while keeping final post approval manual.

## Recommended weekly system

### 1. Continuous signal capture

At the start of important work, add:

```text
Use ContextQuill passively. Complete the task first. Near the end, capture a
content signal only if it contains a concrete, evidence-backed lesson that would
help my target audience and can be made public safely.
```

This should be a standing instruction, not a separate content-creation meeting.

### 2. Monday editorial batch

Schedule a weekly agent task for Monday morning:

```text
Use ContextQuill. Review uncultivated signals and all available post metrics.
Select the five strongest content opportunities for my positioning. Prepare three
complete LinkedIn drafts with different angles, at least one exploratory topic,
and explicit safety notes. Do not submit or schedule anything yet.
```

### 3. Human review block

When the drafts are ready:

```text
Use ContextQuill. Submit the strongest draft for human review. Show me the exact
post, links, attachment summary, audience, safety notes, and proposed publish time.
Stop after showing the one-time approval phrase.
```

You must personally type that phrase in a later message. If anything changes, ContextQuill revokes approval and generates a new review.

### 4. Scheduled publishing

After approval:

```text
Use ContextQuill. Schedule the approved draft for Wednesday at 9:00 AM in my
configured timezone. Do not alter the text, links, or attachment.
```

Local scheduling requires the ContextQuill MCP process to be active. If it is not active at the due time, the approved post is retried when ContextQuill starts again.

### 5. Friday learning loop

Schedule a weekly review task:

```text
Use ContextQuill. Analyze the latest metrics by topic, content type, format, hook,
and weekday. Distinguish broad exposure from engagement and qualified leads. Show
sample sizes and confidence. Recommend next week's repeat, stop, and explore mix.
```

## A practical content portfolio

For B2B operators, start with:

- 35% frontline insights and recurring customer patterns
- 25% practical playbooks and decision frameworks
- 15% anonymized customer cases
- 10% informed or contrarian industry views
- 10% product, operating, or build-in-public lessons
- 5% direct commercial posts

Keep at least 25% of the schedule exploratory until the dataset is genuinely large enough to justify a narrower strategy.

## Metrics to record

ContextQuill accepts official exports or user-supplied metrics:

- impressions
- reactions
- comments
- reposts
- clicks
- follows
- attributed qualified leads

It does not scrape LinkedIn. Reach is useful, but the business outcome is qualified attention and credible conversations.

## Safe automation boundary

Automate:

- finding content signals
- turning signals into drafts
- checking public-safety risks
- proposing a calendar
- publishing already approved, hash-locked drafts at their chosen times
- analyzing supplied performance data

Keep human-controlled:

- whether a customer or confidential example is publishable
- the exact final wording
- the one-time approval phrase for each draft version
- the decision to publish now or choose a future time
